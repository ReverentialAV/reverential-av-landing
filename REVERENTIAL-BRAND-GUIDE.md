# Reverential AV Integration — Brand Guide
## For Carousel, Poster, and Social Content Production

---

## 1. BRAND POSITIONING

**What Reverential does:**
Acoustic design, AV integration and sound system installation for churches, auditoriums and corporate spaces. Measured before installation. Proven before handover.

**What makes it different:**
Every system is measured — not guessed. The founder is a live sound engineer who spent a decade working large-scale productions across India, Saudi Arabia and the UAE before founding Reverential. The work comes from technical rigour and personal conviction, not box-moving.

**Brand character:**
Precise. Authoritative. Restrained. Never loud, never sales-driven. The brand should feel like a premium engineering journal, not an AV retail catalogue.

**Primary audience:**
Church committees and pastors. Auditorium and event space managers. Corporate facility heads. These are decision-makers who have been sold to before and are now looking for someone who can be trusted.

## 2. COLOUR SYSTEM

### Primary Palette

| Role | Hex | Usage |
|------|-----|-------|
| Near-black | `#0B0B0B` | Primary background. The canvas. Most compositions sit on this. |
| Warm cream | `#F1F0EC` | Body text on dark backgrounds. Secondary backgrounds. |
| Gold | `#C6A13A` | Single accent. One key word, one number, one line. Used sparingly. |

### Secondary Palette

| Role | Hex | Usage |
|------|-----|-------|
| Body text | `#4A4A4A` | Body copy on light/cream backgrounds |
| Muted | `#8A8580` | Sub-labels, captions, secondary information |
| Border/line | `#E0DCD3` | Dividers, card borders, subtle separators |

### Colour Rules — Non-Negotiable

- **NO pure white.** Use `#F1F0EC` (warm cream) instead. Pure white reads as generic.
- **NO neon, no bright colours.** No vibrant blue, red, green, purple.
- **NO gradient backgrounds.** Flat colour only — either `#0B0B0B` or `#F1F0EC`.
- **Gold is used once per composition.** Never flood with gold. One highlight per slide.
- **Dark backgrounds dominate.** 80% of compositions should be dark.

## 3. TYPOGRAPHY SYSTEM

### Typefaces

| Role | Typeface | Weight |
|------|---------|--------|
| Headlines | Spectral (serif) | Regular 400 or Light 300 |
| Pull quotes | Spectral Italic | Italic 400 |
| Body / UI | IBM Plex Sans | Regular 400 or Medium 500 |
| Numbers / labels / technical data | IBM Plex Mono | Regular 400 |

**Google Fonts source:**
`https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Spectral`
`https://fonts.google.com/specimen/IBM+Plex+Sans`
`https://fonts.google.com/specimen/IBM+Plex+Mono`

### Type Hierarchy for Carousels

- **Statement (Slide 1 / Title):** Spectral, 48–64pt, Light or Regular, cream on black
- **Key insight (Body):** IBM Plex Sans, 20–24pt, Regular, cream
- **Data / measurement label:** IBM Plex Mono, 14–16pt, Regular, gold
- **Caption / source:** IBM Plex Mono, 11–12pt, muted grey
- **Brand attribution:** "REVERENTIAL™" in IBM Plex Sans 600, 12pt, all caps, gold

### Type Rules

- Headlines: very tight leading (1.05–1.1). Let the letters breathe horizontally, not vertically.
- Never use bold for headlines — Spectral Light carries authority without weight.
- Spectral Italic for quotes from Thomas or client testimony. Nothing else gets italic.
- IBM Plex Mono for any number, measurement, or technical label (RT60, STI, dB).
- Letter-spacing for eyebrow/label text: +50–80 tracking, all caps.

## 4. LAYOUT & COMPOSITION

### Carousel Format

- **Square:** 1080 × 1080px (Instagram grid, LinkedIn)
- **Portrait:** 1080 × 1350px (Instagram Stories preference, LinkedIn mobile)
- **LinkedIn document/PDF carousel:** 1200 × 628px (16:9)

### Grid Principles

- Minimum margins: 64px on all sides (square), 72px portrait
- Never crowd the edges. White (black) space is premium.
- One focal element per slide. One idea. One thing the eye lands on.
- The gold accent should be the last thing placed, not the first.

### Compositional Approaches (use one per slide)

**A — Statement:**
Large Spectral headline filling 60–70% of the canvas. Eyebrow label above in Mono. Brand mark bottom-right or bottom-left.

**B — Data spotlight:**
One number in IBM Plex Mono at very large size (120–180pt), in gold. One line of context in IBM Plex Sans Regular below. Everything else is silence.

**C — Quote:**
Spectral italic, 28–36pt, cream, left-aligned with a 3px gold left border. Attribution below in Mono 12pt.

**D — Diagram / technical:**
Dark background. Clean geometric linework in cream and gold. No clipart. No icons. Architectural precision only.

**E — Contrast pair:**
Two columns: left dark with cream text, right slightly lighter dark with gold label. Used for before/after or comparison slides.

## 5. VISUAL ELEMENTS

### Photography Style (when used)
- Dark, moody, high depth-of-field (natural bokeh)
- Mixing consoles, line arrays, church interiors, technical equipment
- No staged stock photography. No people smiling at cameras.
- People (if any): silhouettes or in-action shots, not portraits

### Diagrams and Technical Illustration
- Gothic arch cross-sections for church acoustics
- Sound paths as clean geometric gold and cream lines
- Equipment rack drawings in clean linework
- No 3D renders. No shadows. No gradients.

### Icons
- Minimal stroke icons only (1.5–2px stroke, no fill)
- Use sparingly — one per slide maximum
- Never use emoji in professional collateral

### Texture / Background Treatment
- Very subtle dark grid overlay is permitted (opacity 8–12%)
- Stone masonry hint lines permitted at ultra-low opacity
- No wood textures, no fabric, no gradients

## 6. BRAND VOICE FOR CAROUSELS

### Tone Principles
- **Direct:** State the insight in the first line. No build-up.
- **Earned authority:** Every claim has a basis. Never "we're the best." Show the thinking.
- **No exclamation marks.** Never.
- **No emojis** in carousel text.
- **Short sentences.** 12 words maximum per line on a slide.
- **Plain English for technical concepts:** RT60 = how long sound rings in a room after it stops.

### Voice Samples — Do This

> "Most churches replace their sound system twice before anyone measures the room."

> "The problem is almost never the equipment."

> "RT60. One number. Everything you need to know about your room."

> "A proposal that lists boxes but predicts no results is a shopping list."

### Voice Samples — Never This

> "Transform your worship experience with cutting-edge AV solutions!"

> "We deliver world-class audio for your space 🎉"

> "Are you struggling with bad sound? We can help!!"

## 7. CAROUSEL STRUCTURE

Every carousel (5–7 slides) follows this arc:

| Slide | Purpose | Element type |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| 1 — Hook | State the problem in one line | Statement (A) |
| 2 — Diagnosis | The real cause most people miss | Statement or Data (A/B) |
| 3 — Evidence | The number, measurement, or fact | Data spotlight (B) |
| 4 — Insight | What it means in plain language | Quote or Statement (C/A) |
| 5 — Method | What the right approach looks like | Diagram or Contrast (D/E) |
| 6 — Proof | Result or validation | Data or Quote (B/C) |
| 7 — CTA | One clear next step | Statement with brand mark |

## 8. BRAND MARK USAGE

### Logo — Always use official files
- Dark background: `logo-white.png`
- Light/cream background: `logo.png` (the gold/dark version)
- Never recreate the logo. Never use text alone to represent the brand.
- Minimum size: 30px height on digital, 15mm on print

### Brand Attribution Line
Every carousel must end with:
```
REVERENTIAL™
AV Acoustics Integration
reverentialav.in
```
In IBM Plex Sans Regular 11pt, muted gold or cream. Bottom-right or bottom-left.

## 10. WHAT REVERENTIAL IS NOT

Never produce content that looks like:
- A nightclub AV company (neon, beats, excitement)
- A church media ministry (crosses, doves, generic worship imagery)
- A generic tech startup (clean white, Poppins/Inter, blue accents)
- A consumer electronics brand (product photography, lifestyle shots)
- A corporate PowerPoint deck (clip-art icons, template layouts)

Reverential sits between premium engineering and premium editorial. 
The closest reference aesthetics: Zeiss photography, Leica brand materials, 
acoustic engineering journals, premium architecture firm presentations.
